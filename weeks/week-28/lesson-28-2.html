📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
How did English-speaking people limit their rulers before America existed?
The Founders did not invent limited government. They inherited it from a long line of documents, each one won from a ruler who did not want to give way. This lesson follows four of them.

A King Pushed Too Far

📚 PART 2 · A Ship and a Revolution13 min

The Mayflower Compact, 1620

In November 1620 a ship called the Mayflower anchored off the coast of what is now Massachusetts. It carried about one hundred people, many of them Pilgrims seeking a place to worship freely. They had meant to settle farther south, in land covered by a royal grant, and now they were outside it. Some passengers said that, with no law over them, they could do as they pleased.

The leaders feared disorder. So forty-one of the men aboard signed a short compactKOM-paktA serious agreement among people to follow certain rules together.. They promised to “combine ourselves together into a civil Body Politic” and to obey the laws and offices they would choose for the good of the colony. No king gave them this government. They made it themselves, by agreement. It is a clear early example of government by consentkun-SENTAgreement. To give consent is to agree to something freely.. The same idea echoes in the opening words of the Constitution: “We the People.”

The Glorious Revolution, 1688

The next step came in England itself. King James II tried to rule without Parliament, to keep a large army in time of peace, and to place his friends in high offices. Many leaders feared that England would return to the rule of an unchecked king. In 1688 they invited a Dutch prince, William of Orange, who was married to James’s Protestant daughter Mary, to come to England with an army. James fled with almost no fighting. Historians call this the Glorious Revolution because it changed the government with so little bloodshed.

It did not have to be peaceful. Had James’s army stood and fought, England might have fallen into a new civil war. Instead Parliament set terms for the new rulers in a law called the English Bill of Rights in 1689. The king could not make laws or collect taxes without Parliament. He could not keep an army in peacetime without Parliament’s consentkun-SENTAgreement. To give consent is to agree to something freely.. Members of Parliament had freedom of speech. Citizens had the right to petition, and punishments could not be “cruel and unusual.” Americans would copy those last words into the Eighth Amendment.

📚 PART 3 · Paine and the Line to America12 min

Common Sense, 1776

By 1776 many colonists still hoped to reconcile with the king. In January a pamphlet appeared called Common Sense. Its author, Thomas Paine, was an English craftsman who had come to America only two years before. He wrote in plain words that ordinary people could follow. He argued that a king was no better than any other man, that it was foolish for a small island to rule a continent, and that it was time for America to govern itself. The pamphlet sold in enormous numbers for its time, and it was read aloud in taverns and army camps. It helped turn a quarrel over taxes into a movement for independence.

A Line From Each

Now trace a line from each of these four steps to the documents of the American founding. Magna CartaMAG-nuh KAR-tuhLatin for “Great Charter.” The document the English barons forced King John to seal in 1215. says that the king must follow the law before punishing a free man. Echo: the due processDOO PROS-essFair and regular legal steps that a government must follow before it takes a person’s life, liberty, or property. clauses of the Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments. The Mayflower CompactKOM-paktA serious agreement among people to follow certain rules together. says that people make a government by agreeing together. Echo: “We the People” in the Preamble. The English Bill of Rights says that a king cannot tax or keep an army without Parliament and that punishments may not be cruel. Echo: the Eighth Amendment, and the power of Congress over taxes and the army. Common Sense says that a people may choose to govern themselves. Echo: the Declaration of Independence.

Notice what all four share. Each was won from a ruler who held more power than he should. Each limited that power by law or by agreement. And each was fragile, since a king could ignore a charter, as John tried to do. The Founders understood that it takes more than a piece of paper. A free people also has to be ready to defend the paper, which is why the Constitution is built the way it is.
