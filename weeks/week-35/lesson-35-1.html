📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
What does a republic owe its own liberty when it deals with the rest of the world?
A republic cannot choose to have no foreign policy. What it does abroad lands at home, in taxes, in soldiers, and in the safety of its citizens. This lesson follows how Americans have argued about their place in the world.

The Monroe Doctrine

In 1823 President James Monroe added a second principle. Spain’s colonies in Latin America had won their independence, and European kings talked of taking them back. Monroe told Congress that the Americas were no longer open to new colonies from Europe. He said the United States would regard any European effort to control the free nations of the Western Hemisphere as dangerous to its own peace and safety. In return, the United States would stay out of Europe’s wars. This statement became known as the Monroe doctrineDOK-trinA statement of the principles a government will follow in a certain area of policy.. At the time the United States had a small navy, and Great Britain’s fleet did more to enforce it. But the idea lasted, and later Presidents used it often.

A New Order After War

The attack on Pearl Harbor in December 1941 brought America into the Second World War. When the war ended in 1945, most Americans did not want to repeat the mistake of walking away. The United States helped found the United Nations that year, with its home in New York. The UN works to keep peace, settle quarrels, and help in disasters. Organization, or NATO, an allianceuh-LY-ensA promise between countries to help defend one another. for mutual defense. Its main promise is that an attack on one member is an attack on all.

The memory of the failed League and of Pearl Harbor changed American opinion. Before the attack, many Americans were isolationistseye-suh-LAY-shun-iz-umThe belief that a country should stay out of other nations’ affairs and conflicts. and wanted to stay out of the war in Europe and Asia. After it, most agreed that the country could not simply stand apart from the world.

The Truman Doctrine

After the war, the Soviet Union and the United States became rivals. In 1947 Greece was fighting a civil war against communist rebels, and Turkey was under pressure from the Soviets. Britain could no longer afford to help them. President Harry Truman asked Congress for money for both countries. He said that the United States must support free peoples who are resisting being forced into a communist system. This became known as the Truman doctrineDOK-trinA statement of the principles a government will follow in a certain area of policy.. Congress approved the aid, and it set the pattern for years of Cold War policy.

The answer to the opening question is still argued. Some say America should lead, because freedom is safer in a world of free nations. Others say America should be careful, because every commitment costs money and lives. A citizen should weigh both.
